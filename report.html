Finetuning ProGen2 on Green Fluorescent Protein

Assignment 1: Top-5 Candidate Report

CMU 11781 / 02741, Generative AI for Biomedicine · Jonah Zembower · September 30, 2026

1. Summary

ProGen2-small (151M parameters) was finetuned on 47,854 green fluorescent protein (GFP) variants for the full 10 epochs at the provided batch size of 32, and used to generate 92 candidate sequences, each conditioned on a 64-residue prompt. Candidates were folded with AlphaFold 3 and ranked by mean pLDDT subject to pTM > 0.8.

Every folded candidate passed the pTM > 0.8 criterion (pTM 0.90–0.92, mean pLDDT 90.70–92.30), indicating that finetuning succeeded: the model produces sequences that AlphaFold 3 confidently folds into the GFP β-barrel, at the correct length of 237 residues, with the chromophore-forming triad intact.

The divergence analysis, however, shows that the finetuned model contributes almost no novel sequence variation. Under greedy decoding it reproduced the training consensus exactly at every generated position across all 92 designs. Under nucleus (top-p) sampling it introduced 5 substitutions across 92 designs, and none of them appears in any of the top-5 candidates: all 9 substitutions in the reported designs were inherited from their prompts. Section 6 argues this follows directly from the structure of the training data.

2. Method

2.1 Data

The training set is 47,854 sequences, each exactly 237 residues. The consensus sequence (most frequent residue per position) corresponds to avGFP with the initial methionine removed, so consensus position i equals avGFP position i+1. The chromophore-forming triad Ser–Tyr–Gly sits at positions 64–66.

This is a point-mutation scan of a single protein rather than a diverse homolog family: mean per-position conservation is 0.984, and no position varies by more than ~4% (minimum conservation 0.959). Six positions (1, 87, 93, 118, 119, 237) never vary at all.

The model converges within one epoch, and the remaining nine are noise.

Validation loss reaches 0.0880 by epoch 3 and improves by only 0.0008 over the following seven epochs. To test whether the epoch-to-epoch movement carries any signal, the identical configuration — same seed, same batch size, same code — was run a second time on a different GPU (NVIDIA H100-80). That replicate's best validation loss was 0.08933, reached at epoch 1, with all nine subsequent epochs worse.

The two runs' best losses differ by 0.00215. The replicate's own epoch-to-epoch range was 0.0339 — roughly 16× larger than the gap between runs. Differences of this size are therefore not evidence that one run learned more than the other; they reflect floating-point non-determinism between GPU architectures. The epoch-7 checkpoint was selected because it had the lowest validation loss of any checkpoint produced, which is an objective rule, not a claim that it is a better model.

The epoch-5 excursion to 0.2118 — roughly 2.3× its neighbours, fully recovered by epoch 6 — is a transient optimisation instability at a learning rate of about 7.3×10−4.

2.3 Generation

Each of the 92 held-out test sequences supplied its first 64 residues as a prompt; the model then generated autoregressively to an EOS token or 512 tokens. Because the prompt ends at position 64, the model itself generates the chromophore tyrosine at position 65. All 92 outputs terminated at exactly 237 residues, confirming the model learned the family's length.

Both decoding strategies were run for comparison (Section 5.4). The reported candidates use nucleus (top-p) sampling, p = 0.8, MAX_SEQ_LENGTH = 512, batch size 1, seed 0.

2.4 Structure evaluation

Twenty candidates were submitted to the AlphaFold 3 web server (alphafoldserver.com) as single protein chains, one copy each, with server-generated MSAs. Ranking used mean pLDDT over all atoms, filtered to pTM > 0.8.

Ranking is over 18 of the 20 folded designs. Why, and what it does not affect.

The 20 structures were folded from an earlier, shorter finetuning run. Comparing that run's 92 top-p designs against this one's, 84 of 92 sequences are byte-identical; 8 differ (designs 6, 11, 28, 45, 48, 65, 73, 83). Two of those 8 — design_006 and design_011 — were among the 20 folded, so their structures no longer correspond to the sequences this model produces. They are excluded. The remaining 18 folded designs are the exact sequences generated by the epoch-7 checkpoint reported here, and all 18 pass pTM > 0.8.

This affects only the fifth-ranked slot. Ranks 1–4 are unchanged, and design_001 (pLDDT 91.57) moves up from sixth to fifth. The excluded design_006 previously ranked fifth at pLDDT 91.81, so the exclusion changes the reported top-5 by 0.24 pLDDT points — well inside the noise floor discussed in Section 6. The unfolded variants of designs 6 and 11 could have ranked anywhere; that is a genuine limitation, recorded in Section 7.

3. What pLDDT and pTM measure

pLDDT (predicted Local Distance Difference Test) is a per-atom confidence estimate on a 0–100 scale. It predicts how well the local environment of each atom would match an experimental structure, so it is fundamentally a local measure: high pLDDT means individual secondary-structure elements and side-chain placements are trustworthy. Above 90 is "very high"; 70–90 is confident; below 50 usually indicates disorder. All candidates here exceed 90 on average, which is why the rendered structures are almost uniformly dark blue.

pTM (predicted Template Modeling score) estimates the TM-score between the prediction and the true structure, on a 0–1 scale. Unlike pLDDT it is global: it asks whether the overall fold and the relative arrangement of distant parts of the chain are correct. A pTM above 0.5 implies the general fold is likely right; above 0.8 indicates high confidence in the complete topology. The assignment's pTM > 0.8 threshold therefore tests that the whole β-barrel is coherent, not merely that local geometry looks tidy.

The accompanying PAE (Predicted Aligned Error) plots show expected positional error in ångströms for every residue pair. Uniformly low (green) PAE, as seen here, means the model is confident about how the entire chain packs together, not just locally.

Important limitation. Both metrics measure predicted structural confidence, not biological function. A GFP variant can fold into a perfect β-barrel and still be non-fluorescent if the chromophore environment is disturbed, since chromophore maturation depends on precise side-chain chemistry that AlphaFold does not model. These scores should be read as a filter that removes sequences which clearly fail to fold, not as evidence of fluorescence.

Greedy decoding reproduced the consensus exactly at every generated position in all 92 designs; the sequences appear diverse only because their prompts differ. Nucleus sampling introduced 5 substitutions in total — 2.6% of the 193 differences observed. Top-p was chosen for the submitted candidates because it is the only configuration in which the model contributes anything of its own.

Greedy output is invariant to training length. The greedy design set produced by this 10-epoch checkpoint is byte-identical to the one produced by a 5-epoch checkpoint trained at batch size 8 — all 92 sequences, character for character. Five additional epochs and a fourfold change in batch size altered the argmax at not one of the 92 × 173 = 15,916 generated positions. Under top-p, the same comparison changes only 8 of 92 designs. Whatever the model learns about this family, it has learned it well before epoch 5.

6. Interpretation

Finetuning unambiguously succeeded at the task it was given: every candidate folds into a confident GFP β-barrel (pTM 0.90–0.92, mean pLDDT > 90), terminates at exactly 237 residues, and preserves the SYG chromophore triad at 64–66 — including the tyrosine at position 65, which the model generates itself since the prompt ends at 64. What the model contributed to each design, however, is essentially the consensus sequence. All nine substitutions in the top-5 candidates were inherited verbatim from their 64-residue prompts, not one was authored by the model, and across the whole 92-design set only 5 of 193 substitutions came from the decoder. This follows directly from the training data: a point-mutation scan of a single protein at 98.4% mean conservation leaves almost no entropy for a converged autoregressive model to sample. The convergence is extreme — validation loss bottoms out by epoch 3, a replicate run on different hardware bottomed out at epoch 1, and the greedy design set is byte-identical to one generated by a checkpoint trained for half as long at a quarter the batch size. The next-residue distribution is so sharp that even nucleus sampling at p = 0.8 usually finds a single residue occupying the entire 80% probability mass, and greedy decoding is fully deterministic. The pLDDT ranking, in turn, distinguishes almost nothing: the top five span just 0.73 pLDDT points (92.30 to 91.57) and all eighteen span 1.60, while the candidates differ from one another by only one to two residues out of 237. Differences that small are comparable to AlphaFold's own run-to-run variation — the same order as the 0.00215 validation-loss gap between two identically configured training runs — so the ordering should be read as noise rather than as a meaningful quality gradient. The honest conclusion is that this pipeline reliably reproduces GFP rather than diversifying it, and that pLDDT is useful here as a pass/fail filter — confirming the sequences are foldable — not as a ranking signal.

7. Limitations and possible extensions

  • Confidence is not function. pLDDT and pTM assess predicted structure. None of these candidates has been tested for fluorescence, which depends on chromophore maturation chemistry AlphaFold does not model.
  • Ranking resolution. With a 0.73-point spread across the top five, pLDDT cannot meaningfully order near-identical sequences. Repeating each fold across several seeds would quantify the noise floor directly.
  • Two designs excluded from ranking. Of the 20 folded structures, design_006 and design_011 correspond to sequences this checkpoint no longer produces, so they were excluded (Section 2.4). Their current variants were never folded and could in principle have ranked anywhere; design_006's superseded variant ranked fifth at pLDDT 91.81. Folding the two replacements would close this gap.
  • Diversity is decoder-limited. Higher p, a sampling temperature above 1.0, or far fewer finetuning updates would all increase novelty — at some cost to the near-perfect fold confidence observed here. Given that the model converges within one epoch, early stopping is the most promising lever: checkpointing after a few hundred updates, before the distribution sharpens, would likely yield more model-authored variation.
  • Subset folded. 18 of 92 candidates were ranked, limited by AlphaFold Server's daily quota. Given that all 18 passed pTM > 0.8, folding the remainder would likely add little — though the 5 designs carrying model-authored substitutions were not among those folded, and are the most interesting ones to test.
