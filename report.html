Finetuning ProGen2 on Green Fluorescent Protein

Assignment 1: Top-5 Candidate Report

CMU 11781 / 02741, Generative AI for Biomedicine · Jonah Zembower · September 29, 2026

1. Summary

ProGen2-small (151M parameters) was finetuned on 47,854 green fluorescent protein (GFP) variants and used to generate 92 candidate sequences, each conditioned on a 64-residue prompt. Twenty candidates were folded with AlphaFold 3 and ranked by mean pLDDT subject to pTM > 0.8.

All 20 folded candidates passed the pTM > 0.8 criterion (pTM 0.90–0.92, mean pLDDT 90.70–92.30), indicating that finetuning succeeded: the model produces sequences that AlphaFold 3 confidently folds into the GFP β-barrel, at the correct length of 237 residues, with the chromophore-forming triad intact.

The divergence analysis, however, shows that the finetuned model contributes almost no novel sequence variation. Under greedy decoding it reproduced the training consensus exactly in every generated position across all 92 designs. Under nucleus (top-p) sampling it introduced just 3 substitutions across 92 designs. Section 6 argues this follows directly from the structure of the training data.

2. Method

2.1 Data

The training set is 47,854 sequences, each exactly 237 residues. The consensus sequence (most frequent residue per position) corresponds to avGFP with the initial methionine removed, so consensus position i equals avGFP position i+1. The chromophore-forming triad Ser–Tyr–Gly sits at positions 64–66.

This is a point-mutation scan of a single protein rather than a diverse homolog family: mean per-position conservation is 0.984, and no position varies by more than ~4% (minimum conservation 0.959). Six positions (1, 87, 93, 118, 119, 237) never vary at all.

Continued training sharpens the output distribution further, which — given the findings in Section 6 — would reduce generation diversity rather than improve candidate quality.

2.3 Generation

Each of the 92 held-out test sequences supplied its first 64 residues as a prompt; the model then generated autoregressively to an EOS token or 512 tokens. Because the prompt ends at position 64, the model itself generates the chromophore tyrosine at position 65. All 92 outputs terminated at exactly 237 residues, confirming the model learned the family's length.

Both decoding strategies were run for comparison (Section 6.3). The reported candidates use nucleus (top-p) sampling, p = 0.8, MAX_SEQ_LENGTH = 512, batch size 1, seed 0.

2.4 Structure evaluation

Twenty candidates were submitted to the AlphaFold 3 web server (alphafoldserver.com) as single protein chains, one copy each, with server-generated MSAs. Ranking used mean pLDDT over all atoms, filtered to pTM > 0.8.

3. What pLDDT and pTM measure

pLDDT (predicted Local Distance Difference Test) is a per-atom confidence estimate on a 0–100 scale. It predicts how well the local environment of each atom would match an experimental structure, so it is fundamentally a local measure: high pLDDT means individual secondary-structure elements and side-chain placements are trustworthy. Above 90 is "very high"; 70–90 is confident; below 50 usually indicates disorder. All candidates here exceed 90 on average, which is why the rendered structures are almost uniformly dark blue.

pTM (predicted Template Modeling score) estimates the TM-score between the prediction and the true structure, on a 0–1 scale. Unlike pLDDT it is global: it asks whether the overall fold and the relative arrangement of distant parts of the chain are correct. A pTM above 0.5 implies the general fold is likely right; above 0.8 indicates high confidence in the complete topology. The assignment's pTM > 0.8 threshold therefore tests that the whole β-barrel is coherent, not merely that local geometry looks tidy.

The accompanying PAE (Predicted Aligned Error) plots show expected positional error in ångströms for every residue pair. Uniformly low (green) PAE, as seen here, means the model is confident about how the entire chain packs together, not just locally.

Important limitation. Both metrics measure predicted structural confidence, not biological function. A GFP variant can fold into a perfect β-barrel and still be non-fluorescent if the chromophore environment is disturbed, since chromophore maturation depends on precise side-chain chemistry that AlphaFold does not model. These scores should be read as a filter that removes sequences which clearly fail to fold, not as evidence of fluorescence.

Greedy decoding reproduced the consensus exactly at every generated position in all 92 designs; the sequences appear diverse only because their prompts differ. Nucleus sampling introduced 3 substitutions in total — 1.6% of the 191 differences observed. Top-p was chosen for the submitted candidates because it is the only configuration in which the model contributes anything of its own.

6. Interpretation

Finetuning unambiguously succeeded at the task it was given: every candidate folds into a confident GFP β-barrel (pTM 0.90–0.92, mean pLDDT > 90), terminates at exactly 237 residues, and preserves the SYG chromophore triad at 64–66 — including the tyrosine at position 65, which the model generates itself since the prompt ends at 64. What the model contributed to each design, however, is essentially the consensus sequence. Nine of the ten substitutions in the top-5 candidates were inherited verbatim from their 64-residue prompts; only N134S in design_006 was authored by the model, and across the whole 92-design set only 3 of 191 substitutions came from the decoder. This follows directly from the training data: a point-mutation scan of a single protein at 98.4% mean conservation leaves almost no entropy for a converged autoregressive model to sample. After five epochs the next-residue distribution is so sharp that even nucleus sampling at p = 0.8 usually finds a single residue occupying the entire 80% probability mass, and greedy decoding is fully deterministic. The pLDDT ranking, in turn, distinguishes almost nothing: the top five span just 0.49 pLDDT points (92.30 to 91.81) and all twenty span 1.60, while the candidates differ from one another by only one to three residues out of 237. Differences that small are comparable to AlphaFold's own run-to-run variation, so the ordering should be read as noise rather than as a meaningful quality gradient. The honest conclusion is that this pipeline reliably reproduces GFP rather than diversifying it, and that pLDDT is useful here as a pass/fail filter — confirming the sequences are foldable — not as a ranking signal.

7. Limitations and possible extensions

  • Confidence is not function. pLDDT and pTM assess predicted structure. None of these candidates has been tested for fluorescence, which depends on chromophore maturation chemistry AlphaFold does not model.
  • Ranking resolution. With a 0.49-point spread across the top five, pLDDT cannot meaningfully order near-identical sequences. Repeating each fold across several seeds would quantify the noise floor directly.
  • Diversity is decoder-limited. Higher p, a sampling temperature above 1.0, or fewer finetuning epochs would all increase novelty — at some cost to the near-perfect fold confidence observed here. Mapping that trade-off is the natural next experiment.
  • Subset folded. 20 of 92 candidates were folded, limited by AlphaFold Server's daily quota. Given that all 20 passed pTM > 0.8, folding the remainder would likely add little.
  • Partial training. Five of ten epochs were run. A fully trained model would likely score marginally higher on pLDDT while generating even less diversity.
