Finetuning ProGen2 on Green Fluorescent Protein

Assignment 1 — Top-5 Candidate Report

CMU 11781 / 02741, Generative AI for Biomedicine · Jonah Zembower · September 30, 2026

1. Setup

ProGen2-small was finetuned on 47,854 GFP sequences of 237 residues each, then prompted with the first 64 residues of each of the 92 held-out test sequences to generate complete proteins using nucleus (top-p) sampling. Candidates were folded with AlphaFold 3 (alphafoldserver.com) and ranked by mean pLDDT subject to pTM > 0.8. All 92 generations terminated at exactly 237 residues.

92 of the 92 designs were folded, limited by the AlphaFold Server's daily job quota; all 92 passed the pTM > 0.8 gate, and the 5 highest by mean pLDDT are reported below.

2. What pLDDT and pTM measure

pLDDT (predicted Local Distance Difference Test) is a per-atom confidence score on a 0–100 scale, reported here as the mean over all atoms. It is a local measure: it predicts how well each atom's immediate environment would match an experimental structure. Above 90 is "very high", 70–90 confident, below 50 usually disordered. Every candidate here averages above 90, which is why the rendered structures are almost uniformly dark blue.

pTM (predicted Template Modeling score) estimates the TM-score between the prediction and the true structure, on a 0–1 scale. Unlike pLDDT it is global: it asks whether the overall fold and the relative placement of distant parts of the chain are right. Above 0.5 means the general fold is probably correct; above 0.8 indicates high confidence in the whole topology. The assignment's pTM > 0.8 gate therefore tests that the complete β-barrel is coherent, not just that local geometry looks tidy.

The PAE (Predicted Aligned Error) panel beside each structure gives expected positional error in ångströms for every residue pair. Uniformly low (green) PAE means the model is confident about how the whole chain packs together.

These metrics measure predicted structural confidence, not function. A GFP variant can fold into a convincing β-barrel and still be dark if the chromophore environment is disturbed, since chromophore maturation depends on side-chain chemistry AlphaFold does not model. They filter out sequences that clearly fail to fold; they do not demonstrate fluorescence.

4.5 Interpretation

Finetuning succeeded at the task it was set: every candidate folds into a confident GFP β-barrel (pTM 0.92–0.92, mean pLDDT above 90), terminates at exactly 237 residues, and preserves the SYG chromophore triad — including the tyrosine at position 65, which the model generates itself because the prompt stops at 64. What the model contributed to each design, however, is essentially the consensus sequence: it added nothing of its own to any of them — all 11 substitutions in the top 5 were handed to it in the prompt. Across all 92 designs the same pattern holds, 188 prompt-region substitutions against 5 from the decoder. This follows from the training data, which is a point-mutation scan of a single protein at 98.4% mean per-position conservation: there is almost no entropy left for a converged autoregressive model to sample, so the next-residue distribution is sharp enough that even nucleus sampling usually finds one residue occupying the entire probability mass. As for what the pLDDT ranking distinguished between — very little. The top 5 span 0.26 pLDDT points while differing from one another by only 1 to 3 residues out of 237. A gap that small is comparable to AlphaFold's own run-to-run variation, so the ordering is better read as noise than as a quality gradient. The defensible conclusion is that pLDDT worked here as a pass/fail filter confirming these sequences are foldable, which is what the pTM > 0.8 gate was for, and not as a way to rank near-identical candidates against each other.
